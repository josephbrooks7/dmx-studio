# Body-preserving garment fitting requirements

Clothing selection must never modify, replace, flatten or masculinize/feminize the selected body mesh, shape keys, skeleton or body settings. The target body is authoritative. Preserve bust/chest projection, pectoral and shoulder shape, waist, hips, abdomen, muscularity and height. Garment design, neckline, hem, seam structure, material slots and UVs remain attributable to the source garment. Fit allowance depends on garment style (fitted versus loose); do not blindly shrink-wrap loose clothing. Use target skin geometry and anatomical landmarks for region-specific deformation, with separate anterior/posterior torso profiles and clearance checks. A target shirt cage alone is not proof of anatomical fidelity. Do not transfer source body shape or baked anatomy through clothing. Avoid hiding body geometry merely to conceal clipping unless a separately verified covered-skin mask is required; never change the visible silhouette.

Acceptance: compare source and target body geometry/settings before and after wardrobe changes; require unchanged body vertex positions and shape parameters. Test the same tee on multiple chest/bust, shoulder, waist and hip profiles, including muscular and fuller-bust characters. Check front, side and three-quarter views, neckline, chest projection, seams, skin intersections, and posed deformation. Compare fitted and loose styles separately. Do not enable experimental garments in the production wardrobe until visual and geometric checks pass. Existing bidirectional tee projection trials are experimental and do not establish these requirements.
